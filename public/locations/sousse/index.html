Wedding photographer in Sousse

Yesser Barka is a wedding photographer based in Sousse, Tunisia. Sousse is his home, not a city he drives in for. He photographs weddings in the medina, along the Boujaafar corniche, in Hammam Sousse, and at Port El Kantaoui. A Sousse date does not need a hotel night beforehand, because he is already in the city.

Who photographs weddings in Sousse?

Yesser Barka does, from Sousse. Couples searching for a photographe mariage à Sousse are reaching the person whose name is on the photographs, not an agency that sends whoever is free. The published Sousse story on this site is Erij and Bechir.

There is no separate studio address to visit, and no phone number is published on this site. Booking is the inquiry form: date, venue, and guest count. Instagram is @yesser.barka.photography.

Which parts of the Sahel are covered from Sousse?

Most Sousse weddings stay inside a short radius, which is the practical difference from a capital wedding:

  • The medina of Sousse, a UNESCO old town, for portraits in lanes around the ribat when the couple wants stone and shade rather than a hotel lobby. Midday in those lanes is dark. The usable hour is late afternoon, when a slice of sun reaches the walls.
  • Port El Kantaoui, the marina north of the city, about a quarter of an hour away. Many Sahel weddings keep preparations, dinner, and the party inside one resort complex, so the photographer is not packing the car at sunset.
  • Hammam Sousse and the Boujaafar corniche, between the medina and the port. The sea here faces east.
  • Monastir is about twenty minutes south when a family asks for portraits near the waterfront there. It is still a Sousse-based day, not a second trip.

Why does the sea light in Sousse not match Tunis?

The Sahel coast faces east. Sunrise is the bright hour on the water, and it is harsh by nine in summer. Sunset does not drop into the sea in Sousse. It falls inland, over the city and the olive plain, so an evening portrait on the beach is front-lit by a low sun behind the photographer, not a silhouette against the Gulf. In Tunis, Sidi Bou Said’s white walls and the northeast-facing Gulf create the opposite problem: reflected noon light, and a drive between neighbourhoods that can eat the only soft hour.

If the couple wants water behind them at sunset, Sousse is the wrong coast for that picture. Say so before the date, and the portrait plan moves to late-day shade in the medina or to open shade at the marina instead of a promise the shoreline cannot keep.

What changes because the photographer lives here?

Getting ready can start in a family house in the city and end in a ballroom at El Kantaoui without a two-hour transfer. There is no dawn departure from another governorate. If the first look is at 9am, he is there at 9am. If the party runs late, the drive home is across Sousse, not down the A1.

What he still needs in writing is the same as any other date: where the morning starts, where dinner is, and whether portraits are in the medina, on the corniche, or inside the hotel. A Sousse wedding that adds a surprise stop in Monastir at 6pm loses the medina light. Put the stop in the note when you book.
